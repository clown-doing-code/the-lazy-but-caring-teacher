**Abstract:** In a world where **AI** *(unless you have been living under rock, I'll assume that you are familiar with the concept of **Artificial Intelligence**)* or **SI** *([Super Intelligence as Trump proclaims](https://www.foxnews.com/live-news/ai-leaders-trump-meeting-google-executive-order))* is taking over, the line that divides the job of a teacher from manageable to challening is becoming blury. Not because AI is taking our jobs as per many people say, but mostly because students, somehow are taking advantage of AI in way that teachers who are not catching up with this technology, are being outperformed; ironically not by peers. This writing has not intention to shame on students as technology is meant to be used, but the main argument of this writing is to analyze how education is mutating; from the perspective of a teacher and from the observations of a educational project carried out at my workplace where students were the focus. 

## Background
To understand this [pesudo-analysis](https://www.dictai.org/w/pseudoanalytical), it's important to highlight the background, what was measured and how, edge cases and what output was obtained to come up with such conclusions. First of, the main source of information was taken from project carried during three weeks; in which ESL intermediate students were asked to write an essay. For most of them, it was the first time in touch with such concept as not even in their [L1](https://www.teachingenglish.org.uk/professional-development/teachers/teaching-knowledge-database/d-h/l1) was something they were familiar with. Moreover, some difficulties were evident at first glance as students are learning a new language, it was quite a issue to take them from zero to writing an essay in English; hence, some were doubtful and skeptical about it. However, it all began either way; theory was taught and thus practice followed up. Every day, for an hour or so, step by step, students were building an essay from scrath. Since a classroom is a controlled enviroment, the topics were assigned by the teacher beforehand to avoid any unforeseen event. And thus, the basis of writing an essay were settled down.


## The Process of Writing from the Student's Perspective

Generally speaking, the process of writing refers to the steps in order to produce an organized, well-structured writing.

Having witnessed each of their writing day by day was the main motivation for this writing. Notes and feedback were given, but also set aside the data collected to tell the story. Observing their weaknesses, strengths and constraints made me realized the scale of the task they were given. I'm not a big fan of emotions during the process of learning but some kind of pity flourished. So midway first week I decided to start over to take them step by step, idea by idea in the process of writing an essay. I asked them to first brainstorm what they have in mind when they thought about the chosen topic, to write not correct sentences but small ideas they could relate, etc. Basically, I broke down into the smallest digestible pieces they could afford.
